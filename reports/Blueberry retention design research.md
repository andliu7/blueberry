# Fix the seams before adding delight

The main problem in Blueberry today is not a missing feature. Every new visitor is sent down one path, and that path is broken. **The home page's only call to action sends first-time users into the game's pathway, and the pathway nodes link to un-namespaced hashes (`#/trainer?…`, `#/lesson/…`) that the site router renders as "404 Looks like you're lost". It still charges the user energy for the attempt** (`src/game/tabs/pathway/PathwayTab.tsx:934-943`; confirmed by running the app and by re-reading the code) [Certain]. Behind that bug sits the bigger issue. Blueberry is two products sharing a domain. It has two onboarding funnels, two flashcard systems, two mascots with two names, two type systems, two progress stores and two "Ask Blueberry" states, and the only thing the halves share is the `theme` localStorage key [Certain]. None of the four reference products gets its pull from having many borrowed mechanics. Each one does a single thing relentlessly well. Duolingo removes the decision of what to do next and makes the daily habit cheap to keep. Anki turns a curated deck into a daily obligation. ALEKS makes a visible completion gap and treats first success as provisional. Alchemie makes the electron the thing you touch. Blueberry already has code for all four, and that is the trap: it reads like a collection because nothing ranks the mechanics and nothing joins them. The plan below is mostly fixing, merging and cutting, with a short build list at the end. One caveat applies throughout: **most competitor pages were blocked by this environment's network proxy**, so many external figures come from search-result excerpts rather than full-page reads. They are tagged, and the load-bearing ones are listed for checking at source.

## The front door leads to a 404, and two products share one domain

Start with what a first-time student actually experiences, because that is where retention is won or lost. The hero's "Get Started" goes to a navy "SYSTEM" window (a third visual style, framed on Solo Leveling per `src/components/EntryGate.tsx:15`). That window promises "**Two quick questions** about your course, then you are straight into it" (`src/components/EntryGate.tsx:234`) [Certain]. What follows is **8 onboarding steps plus an 8-item placement quiz**, and the placement includes general-chemistry items (pH of 0.0100 M HCl, dilution, limiting reagent) for a student who just said they are in Orgo II (`src/game/onboarding/flow.ts:43-169`; observed) [Certain]. Every one of those screens shows the banner "**[HUMAN GATE] Placeholder copy, pending owner review.**" (`src/game/onboarding/copy.ts:179`) [Certain]. The student then lands on the pathway, taps the first node, and gets the site's 404 page, while the game journal records `node_started` and charge drops from 30 [Certain for the first node; Likely for all nodes, since every non-beat node goes through the same `hrefForPlayable` and beat nodes produce `#/lesson/…`, which also 404s]. Every game screen's browser tab reads "**blueberry · Not Found**" because `titleForRoute` has no `app` entry (`src/lib/useDocumentTitle.ts:16-57`) [Certain]. The fix for the core loop is a one-line prefix change. Until it ships, the rest of this report does not matter.

The structural problem underneath is duplication, and the audit maps it cleanly onto the four inspirations. The site carries Anki's *interface* (18 self-rated decks, 581 cards, Review / Almost / Got It with no interval scheduling, `src/App.tsx:138-142`). The game carries Anki's *scheduler* (an SM-2-lite queue with Again/Hard/Good/Easy, `src/game/cards/scheduler.ts:1-50`), and the game's Cards tab contains none of the site's 18 decks [Certain]. The site's `#/start` funnel asks for an exam date and topics and never asks for a daily goal. The game's funnel asks for a daily goal and never asks for an exam date. Neither reads the other's answers (`blueberry_goals_v1` versus `blueberry.progress.v2`) [Certain]. The site mascot says "Hi, I'm Blueberry" (`src/components/StartPage.tsx:278`); the game mascot, a different drawing with a star crown and blush, says "Hi! I'm Berry" (`src/game/onboarding/copy.ts:62`) [Certain]. Nothing on the home page reflects game progress, the game has no link back to the site (its wordmark is an `aria-hidden` span, `src/game/app/Shell.tsx:360-361`), and a returning student has three separate places to resume: decks, the game and the dashboard [Certain].

Other contradictions will eventually cost trust once real students notice them. The Plans section promises Members "Progress that follows you between machines" (`src/components/ui/subscription-plans.tsx:64-74`). No client code writes learning progress to Supabase, and the tables built for it (`mechanism_attempts`, `user_decks`, `user_cards`) have no reads or writes. Meanwhile the Terms page tells users their data never leaves the browser [Certain]. The unpriced Pro tier lists "Mechanism practice, marked as you draw it" as a paid feature (`:83`), but the game's Train tab gives it away with no account [Certain]. The home page runs a carousel of six invented testimonials that labels itself "(these are fake testimonials)" (`src/components/ui/testimonials.tsx:117`). A comment in your own `founding-seats.tsx:7-12` argues that invented reviews are "the single fastest way to lose somebody." I agree with the comment and not with the carousel. The game also shows students engineering-status copy: "arrives in Phase 6", "waiting on a piece of server it would be dishonest to ship without", "Development surface, not a tab: see docs/MASCOT.md" [Certain]. That candour is sincere, but a student reads it as an unfinished product, not as a voice [Likely].

## Each competitor sticks for one mechanism, not a feature list

Taken together, the research shows that the four products retain people through different, narrow mechanisms, and that the famous gamification is secondary to them. The table compresses this. The paragraphs after it explain which parts transfer to a voluntary orgo study tool that no instructor assigns.

| Product | The one thing that creates pull | What makes it cohere | Where it hurts users |
|---|---|---|---|
| Duolingo | A single linear path with one glowing "next" node, plus a streak that costs one lesson to keep | Everything derived from Duo: palette, typeface, shapes, voice | Guilt notifications, Energy paywall friction, less learner choice |
| Anki | A finite daily due queue that feels like a debt, over curated exam-matched decks | Nothing visual. The algorithm and the med-school deck ecosystem hold it together | Dated UI, no onboarding, backlog snowball after missed days |
| ALEKS | A pie showing the unfilled part of the whole course, with "learned" separated from "mastered" | Tied to the gradebook and the deadline | Surprise knowledge checks erase visible progress. Effective but disliked |
| Alchemie | Direct manipulation: you drag electrons and the structure re-forms live | Founder-teacher story, one gesture language, research papers | Little long-term loop (stars per puzzle). Frustration for weaker students |

**Duolingo's core retention job is removing decision cost and lowering the cost of keeping the habit.** The November 2022 path replaced the branching skill tree with one linear route and folded spaced practice and Stories into it. Duolingo says learners on the path had better reading and listening outcomes, not only more engagement ([Duolingo Blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design/)) [Likely; from a search extract, no effect sizes given]. Its best-evidenced streak wins all make the streak *cheaper*, never higher-stakes. Extending a streak with one lesson and showing the XP goal separately gave **+3.3% D14 retention and +1% DAU** ([Duolingo Blog: Improving the streak](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak/)). Letting learners hold two freezes gave +0.38% DAU ([Duolingo Blog: streak habit](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit/)) [Likely; search extract of a primary source]. The retention PM, in a podcast transcript read in full, says two free freezes at streak start was "a huge win", that three freezes did no better than two, that "Earn Back" beat paid Streak Repair, and that gains flatten after day 7, once loss aversion takes over ([Shuttleworth transcript](https://github.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/blob/main/episodes/jackson-shuttleworth/transcript.md)) [Certain that he said it; Likely that it transfers]. The lesson for Blueberry is not "add a streak", which the game already has. It is "make the first screen one tap from a lesson, and make the habit cheap." Duolingo's cohesion is also instructive. Even Duolingo grew tabs that "didn't feel like part of the same family", and it fixed them with one shared header framework rather than new features ([Duolingo Blog: core tabs](https://blog.duolingo.com/core-tabs-redesign/)) [Likely].

**Anki sticks because the content is canonical and the queue is an obligation.** The learning science is the strongest evidence in this whole report: Dunlosky et al. rated only practice testing and distributed practice "high utility" among ten techniques ([APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html)) [Certain]. Med-school stickiness, though, comes mostly from the AnKing deck matched to Step 1, which removes the costly step of making cards, not from Anki's UI ([AnkiHub](https://www.ankihub.net/step-deck)) [Likely; the adoption numbers are vendor claims]. Anki's failure mode is the week-3 backlog. The manual warns that workload above 97% desired retention becomes "overwhelming", and Anki 24.11 added load balancing and Easy Days to smooth pile-ups ([Anki manual source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ankitects/anki-manual/main/src/deck-options.md); [Anki 24.11 release](https://github.com/ankitects/anki/releases/tag/24.11)) [Certain]. The transferable insight is uncomfortable for Blueberry. **Your 581 cards and 43 checked reactions, written for one specific course, are your AnKing.** They currently sit in a deck viewer with no scheduler, disconnected from the only queue that could make them a daily habit.

**ALEKS compels through a visible completion gap and provisional mastery, and it gets away with punishment only because it is graded.** The pie shades mastered topics dark, learned topics light, and leaves the remaining space empty ([McGraw Hill: What is the ALEKS Pie?](https://www.mheducation.com/support/aleks-support-center/knowledge/what-is-the-aleks-pie.html)) [Likely]. A topic is "learned" after reaching 5 points: +1 per correct answer, +2 for consecutive correct answers without opening the explanation, −1 per miss. It is not "mastered" until a later Knowledge Check confirms retention ([ALEKS Knowledge Checks overview](https://www.aleks.com/resources/ALEKS_Knowledge_Checks_Overview_for_Students.pdf)) [Likely]. A propensity-matched study of 6,114 pairs found ALEKS raised the odds of a higher final grade for average, below-average and failing gen-chem students, while students rated it *lower* than traditional homework (3.15 vs 3.31 out of 5) ([ERIC EJ1178678](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1178678)) [Likely]. That "effective but disliked" gap is the key point. A voluntary app has no grade lever, so it cannot copy ALEKS's surprise re-checks that visibly remove progress. Students will simply leave [Likely].

**Alchemie's moat is interaction design and a single origin story, not retention.** Mechanisms makes students push electrons with their fingers, and the app "responds immediately using graphical and audio feedback" as the structure re-forms ([J. Chem. Educ. 2020](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jchemed.0c00246)) [Likely]. Beyond per-puzzle stars, though, the researchers found no streaks, spaced review or daily loop. The app sits around **3.2/5 from 67 US ratings**, and a reviewer asks for a "repeat progress / clean up structure" button to replay the electrons ([App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mechanisms-organic-chemistry/id1157056029)) [Likely; snippet, date unknown]. Alchemie is also the cautionary case for Blueberry's shape: four separate apps (Chairs!, Mechanisms, ModelAR, Animator), held together only by a founder-teacher story and research papers, that have since become a B2B accessibility supplier to WileyPLUS and Learnosity ([Alchemie](https://www.alchem.ie/)) [Likely]. The white space the market research points to is exactly the combination Blueberry has half-built: **direct mechanism practice that explains why an arrow is wrong, inside a Duolingo-grade habit loop**. No competitor found does both [Likely].

## Blueberry reads as a collection because nothing picks a winner

The honest diagnosis is that Blueberry has not decided which half is the product. The code has already partly decided. The home CTA funnels into the game, the EntryGate comment records "Owner direction: one placement quiz, not two" (`src/components/EntryGate.tsx:76-84`), and every retention mechanic (streak, XP, daily goal, quests, spaced repetition, mastery decay) exists only in the game [Certain]. But the site still holds the content, the account system, the course calendar, the office hours and the brand look, and it keeps re-offering its own funnel through a release banner on Study Decks (`src/data/releases.ts:66-80`) [Certain]. My position: **the game is the product, and the site should become its front page and reference library.** The evidence is Duolingo's own consolidation. Stories and practice got little use until they moved onto the main path ([Duolingo Blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design/)) [Likely], and side surfaces that are not on the main line stay unused [Guessing for Blueberry specifically, since you have no usage data].

The visual split follows from the same indecision. The site uses Fraunces, Inter and Caveat, blue-to-indigo gradient buttons, photographic backgrounds and a lowercase serif "blueberry." wordmark. The game uses a system/rounded display stack and flat raised-edge buttons, and its tokens file states "Nothing here is a gradient on a button" (`src/game/tokens.css:11`) and records Fraunces/Inter as SUPERSEDED (`:22-28`) [Certain]. Duolingo's cohesion recipe is to derive palette, typeface and illustration from one mascot, use one display face only for impact moments and one body face, never mix them in one sentence, and apply one voice to UI, notifications and marketing ([Canny Creative brand atlas](https://www.canny-creative.com/atlas/brand/duolingo/); [design.duolingo.com: Voice](https://design.duolingo.com/writing/voice)) [Likely; design.duolingo.com itself was blocked]. Brilliant's 2023 refresh did the same thing on purpose, treating icon, wordmark, illustration style and the Blorb mascot as one system ([Peter Cho](https://pcho.medium.com/a-brilliant-brand-refresh-4af021c11486)) [Likely]. I would make the game's documented token system canonical and restyle the marketing page to match, because students spend their time in the game and it is the half with written rules. That means giving up the editorial serif look you have clearly invested in, which is a real cost [Guessing on your taste; Likely on the reasoning].

The last seam is positioning. Course names drift across surfaces: "CHEM 242 lab LCTAs" in the README, "CHEM241, Summer II" on a home tile, "ORGANIC CHEMISTRY II · CHEM241" on Lessons, generic "Organic Chemistry II" with 5 of 6 courses "Soon" in the game [Certain]. The Anki and ALEKS evidence both say pull comes from being matched to a specific high-stakes exam: AnKing to Step 1, ALEKS to the instructor's deadline. RemNote's exam-date mode compresses intervals before the test ([RemNote](https://www.remnote.com/feature/spaced-repetition)) [Likely]. Your unfair advantage as a solo developer is being in the course: real exam dates in `course_dates`, named TAs, content checked against the actual syllabus. I would lean into "the Maryland orgo companion" now and generalize later, rather than showing five greyed-out courses [Guessing; a positioning judgment, not something the research proves].

## What not to copy, even though it works for them

**Do not gate study behind energy.** The game's "charge" (cap 30, one point per 30 minutes, `packages/economy/src/rules.ts:120-148`) [Certain] is Duolingo's April 2025 Energy system in miniature, and that change was received as a "cash grab" that drains even on perfect lessons ([Class Central](https://www.classcentral.com/report/duolingo-breaks-hearts-for-energy/)) [Likely]. Duolingo itself told shareholders in 2026 that DAU growth had slowed partly because of its monetization focus, and it shifted priority to "teaching better and user growth" ([Q2 2026 letter](https://investors.duolingo.com/static-files/3c8277ee-bc94-4f5d-9b77-0db3e46f88b8)) [Likely]. In orgo, attempts and mistakes are how mechanisms are learned. A meter that makes a student stop practising the night before an exam works directly against your purpose. I would cut charge outright rather than tune it [Likely].

**Do not build leagues, guilt notifications or bandit-optimized reminders.** Leagues work at Duolingo because it can match 30-person cohorts on prior-week engagement; Mazal credits them with +17% learning time ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)) [Likely, extract]. With a few hundred users the brackets will be empty or unfair. Your Leaderboards flag should stay off, and its "not open yet" screen should disappear rather than tease. The bandit notification paper's +0.5% DAU needed millions of sends ([Yancey & Settles, KDD 2020](https://research.duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf)) [Certain about the paper], and the guilt copy critics quote ("These reminders don't seem to be working") is the reputational cost ([Opinions and Conditions](https://opinionsandconditions.substack.com/p/duolingo-owl-dark-patterns-digital-guilt)) [opinion]. One well-written evening "streak saver" email, sent only to students who opted in, is enough [Guessing].

**Do not copy ALEKS's surprise reassessments or its long diagnostic.** The top complaints are knowledge checks that "erase hours of progress" and time burden ([Finish My Math Class](https://finishmymathclass.com/i-hate-aleks-reddit-complaints/)) [low-reliability commercial source, but consistent with the peer-reviewed attitude gap]. Your mastery half-life decay is sound as spacing, but it should show as a "needs review" fade, never as progress disappearing. The placement should be optional or inferred from the first sessions, not an 8-item wall before the first lesson [Likely].

**Do not copy Anki's configuration surface or a lone due count.** Anki's week-3 collapse comes from backlog dread ([Control-Alt-Backspace](https://controlaltbackspace.org/catch-up/), snippet only) [Likely]. Once the site's 581 cards feed a real queue, cap daily reviews, pause new cards when overdue work piles up, order catch-up reviews by most at risk, and pair "due today" with a growth number. **Do not reward volume.** XP for taps and diamonds for a 0-of-1 lesson teach students to farm easy nodes. Math Academy's "1 XP ≈ 1 minute of focused work" is the better model ([dgore review](https://drgore.substack.com/p/my-review-of-math-academy)) [Likely]. **Do not fabricate social proof or outcome stats.** That covers the fake testimonials, and also Brilliant's "6x more effective" style of claim, until you have your own data. The Duolingo "7x more likely to finish" framing only works because the stat is real [Certain that you have no such data yet]. Finally, **do not become Alchemie's four-app suite** by adding more surfaces. Blueberry is already two apps.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. You asked what makes these products addictive. The more useful answer is that **each one earns its pull from one mechanism it will not compromise, and wraps everything else around that mechanism in one visual and verbal system.** Blueberry has implemented something from each of them, but it has not chosen its mechanism. The evidence points to one: **explained, hands-on mechanism practice on the syllabus of a real course, scheduled against real exam dates.** No competitor combines Alchemie's direct manipulation, Anki's curated exam-matched queue, ALEKS's honest learned-versus-retained distinction and Duolingo's cheap daily habit, and each of those already exists in your repository in fragments. The work that makes Blueberry feel like one company is mostly subtraction and joining, not invention.

The second implication is about trust, which a one-person product cannot afford to spend. Duolingo spent it on Energy and guilt and then had to reverse course publicly. ALEKS can spend it only because a gradebook stands behind it. Blueberry's current liabilities are small but visible: a 404 on the first tap, fake reviews on the home page, a paid-tier promise that the code does not keep, and development notes addressed to the student. Fixing those this week will likely matter more to retention than any animation in the catalog [Guessing on magnitude, Certain on the defects]. Once they are fixed, the celebrations are worth building, because by then they will celebrate something that works.
