Blueberry deck format

How to write a .txt file that Blueberry will turn into a deck. Print this page to save it as a PDF.

The shape of the file

A deck file has two parts. First a short header of key: value lines describing the deck, then a line containing only ---, then the questions. 

title: Example: Distillation
short: Distillation
blurb: Fractional versus simple, and reading a distillation curve.
motif: reflux
---
Q: Why does the thermometer bulb sit level with the side arm?
A: So it reads the vapor temperature rather than the liquid's.

Q: What is the azeotrope of ethanol and water?
A: 95% ethanol, boiling at 78.2 C.

Questions are separated from each other by a blank line. That is the only separator, so never leave a blank line in the middle of one question.

Header keys

Only title is required. Everything else has a sensible default.

KeyWhat it doesDefault
title Required. The full name, shown on the deck card. none
short A brief name for the navigation pill, where the full title will not fit. the title, trimmed
blurb One sentence under the title on the hub card. "N questions, uploaded from a text file."
motif Which illustration the card wears. See the list below. erlenmeyer
from, to Hex colours for the card's gradient, e.g. #4338ca. indigo into violet
id The URL the deck lives at. Republishing with the same id replaces the old version rather than adding a second copy. made from the title

Valid motif values: erlenmeyer, benzene, reflux, sepfunnel, ir, nmr, attack, resonance, pka, mascot.

Plain question and answer

One Q: line and one A: line. The answer may run onto as many following lines as you like; they are joined into one paragraph. 

Q: Why must the glassware be oven dried?
A: To drive off adsorbed water on the glass surface, which would
   otherwise quench the moisture sensitive Grignard reagent.
The question must be one line. Only the Q: line itself becomes the question. Anything written between Q: and A: that is not an option is dropped silently, so keep the question on a single line however long it gets.

Multiple choice

Add option lines between the question and the answer. A dash marks a wrong option, an asterisk marks the right one. The A: line is then the explanation shown after someone chooses. 

Q: Which layer holds the product during the extraction?
- The aqueous layer, because the alcohol is polar
* The ether layer, because the product is more soluble there
- Neither, the product stays at the interface
A: The ether layer. Ether is less dense than water, so it is also the
   top layer in the funnel.
The marker needs a space after it. * The ether layer is an option; *The ether layer is not, and will be read as part of the answer. The same goes for -.

Select all that apply

Mark two or more options with * and the card switches behaviour on its own. Options then toggle on and off instead of locking in, and the answer stays hidden behind a Check answer button, so the first click does not give the game away. 

Q: Which of the following are true of Grignard reagents? Select all that apply.
* They are destroyed by water
- They are stable enough to store for months
* They act as strong bases as well as nucleophiles
- They add to alkenes readily
A: A and C. The carbon-magnesium bond is polarized enough to make that
   carbon both a strong nucleophile and a strong base.

Chemistry, formatting and emphasis

Formulas and maths

Wrap LaTeX in single dollar signs and it will be typeset properly. 

Q: What is the density of diethyl ether?
A: About $0.71\ g/mL$, which is why it forms the top layer over water
   at $1.0\ g/mL$.

Useful pieces: $H_2SO_4$ for subscripts, $^{13}C$ for superscripts, $\delta^-$ for partial charges, $35^\circ C$ for degrees, $\approx$ and $\rightarrow$.

Bold

A little inline HTML is allowed in questions and answers. The built-in decks bold the key phrase of every answer, which is what makes a deck skimmable on a second pass. 

A: It <strong>protonates</strong> the magnesium alkoxide into a neutral alcohol.
Scripts are stripped. Anything executable is removed before the deck is saved: <script>, <iframe>, onclick handlers and javascript: links. Ordinary formatting tags come through fine.

Before you upload

  • Save as plain text, .txt. Not .docx, and not rich text.
  • Straight quotes are safest. Word likes to turn " into curly quotes.
  • The --- line cannot be the very first line of the file. Put the header above it.
  • The upload screen parses the file in your browser and shows you the first three cards exactly as students will see them, along with any warnings, before anything is sent. Read the warnings; a skipped block is almost always a missing A: or a stray blank line.

Not supported yet

This format makes question-and-answer decks. The reference decks, the ones laid out as a table of rows with a diagram on hover such as the pKa and IR charts, cannot be written as a text file yet. They need a different shape of data, and adding it is a separate piece of work.
