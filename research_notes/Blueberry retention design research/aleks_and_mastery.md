# ALEKS (McGraw Hill) and Mastery-Based Adaptive Systems — Structure, Adaptivity, Motivation, and Lessons for Blueberry

All findings below come from web-search result snippets of the cited pages, not full-page reads. Snippets are short and can lose context, so treat exact numbers as "as stated in the page snippet." ## 1. How ALEKS decides what to show next: knowledge space theory, the initial assessment, what the student sees

### Takeaway
ALEKS models a course as a set of topics plus a (very large) family of feasible "knowledge states" (which subsets of topics a student could know, given prerequisites). An adaptive Initial Knowledge Check of under 30 questions estimates the student's state; the "outer fringe" of that state (topics whose prerequisites are all known) becomes the menu of "Ready to Learn" topics shown in the pie. Everything else is locked.

### Cited Findings
- Knowledge space theory (KST) is "an approach to the assessment of knowledge based on a combinatoric and probabilistic model introduced by Doignon and Falmagne (1985)." — [Falmagne, "The Assessment of Knowledge, in Theory and in Practice" (aleks.com)](https://www.aleks.com/about_aleks/Science_Behind_ALEKS.pdf); [ALEKS: Research Behind ALEKS – KST](https://www.aleks.com/about_aleks/knowledge_space_theory)
- A knowledge structure (Q, K) is a finite domain of items Q plus a family K of knowledge states; the empty set and the full domain are both states. In a "learning space," any state is fully specified by its inner fringe and outer fringe. — [Falmagne & Doignon, "Knowledge Spaces and Learning Spaces" (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.06757)
- The outer fringe of a state K is the set of items p such that K + {p} is also a state; it "may be thought of as the set of items the student is 'ready to learn.'" The inner fringe is the "high points" of what the student knows (items that can be removed and still leave a valid state). — [Falmagne, Science Behind ALEKS](https://www.aleks.com/about_aleks/Science_Behind_ALEKS.pdf); [Matayoshi et al., "A practical perspective on KST: ALEKS and its data" (preprint)](https://jmatayoshi.github.io/publications/JMP2021_KST_ALEKS_preprint.pdf)
- ALEKS = "Assessment and LEarning in Knowledge Spaces"; it is a web-based intelligent tutoring system built on KST for mathematics, chemistry, statistics and accounting. — [Matayoshi et al., J. Math. Psych. 2021 (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022249621000134)
- The assessment is adaptive: item choice depends on prior answers, and data from previously administered ALEKS assessments are used to estimate the population distribution over knowledge states. — [Matayoshi, "A Data-Based Simulation Study of Reliability for an Adaptive Assessment Based on KST" (IJAIED)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40593-019-00176-0)
- McGraw Hill researchers used data from 700,000+ real ALEKS math assessments to study reliability, and a dataset of complete learning/assessment profiles of 6,701,233 students (2016–2019). — [Matayoshi, IJAIED](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40593-019-00176-0); [Matayoshi et al., J. Math. Psych. 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022249621000134)
- Knowledge spaces used in practice are "far too large for standard statistical counting and estimation techniques," requiring MCMC/Gibbs-sampling methods just to count states. — [Matayoshi 2022, Br. J. Math. Stat. Psych.](https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bmsp.12257)
- ALEKS has studied a recurrent-neural-network-based stopping algorithm to decide when an adaptive assessment can end. — [Matayoshi et al., "Are We There Yet?" (IJAIED)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40593-021-00240-8)
- The Initial Knowledge Check is "less than 30 questions" and "determines the student's unique knowledge state." Students first see a sample problem with quick tips on the I DON'T KNOW button and calculator. — [ALEKS Student Module Reference Guide (aleks.com, 2016)](https://www.aleks.com/highered/math/New_ALEKS_Student_Module_Reference_Guide.pdf)
- Once the Initial Knowledge Check is done, the student sees an ALEKS Pie depicting their knowledge state; topics they are "ready to learn" appear in the pie slices. — [ALEKS Student Module Reference Guide](https://www.aleks.com/highered/math/New_ALEKS_Student_Module_Reference_Guide.pdf)
- Topics are locked when students are not ready for them; "Ready to Learn" = not learned yet, but prerequisites are in place. — [McGraw Hill, ALEKS FAQ (search snippet)](https://www.mheducation.com/prek-12/support/knowledge/aleks-general-tech-questions.html)
- Instructor advice: use "I don't know" only when you really have no idea, "Otherwise, ALEKS will think you don't know a topic that you actually do know and take you back and make you 'learn' them." — [UCSB chemistry course ALEKS instructions](https://people.chem.ucsb.edu/feldwinn/darby/ALEKS.htm)
- ALEKS offers Organic Chemistry 1 and Organic Chemistry 2 as higher-ed science course products (e.g., OChem 1 includes "Substitution and Elimination Reactions of Alcohols," predicting products of alcohol dehydration, drawing alcohol reaction mechanisms), with drawing tools for chairs and complex structures "without stamps." — [ALEKS Course Products: Organic Chemistry 1](https://www.aleks.com/highered/science/course_products?cmscache=detailed&detailed=gscience13_ochema); [McGraw Hill ALEKS Chemistry](https://www.mheducation.com/highered/digital-products/aleks/for-chemistry/chemistry.html)
- For comparison, Math Academy's adaptive placement diagnostic takes about 30–45 minutes and places the student at their "knowledge frontier" (its term for the edge of mastery / zone of proximal development). — [Math Academy, How it works](https://www.mathacademy.com/how-it-works); [The Math Academy Way (PDF)](https://www.justinmath.com/files/the-math-academy-way.pdf)

### Inferences
- The "what next" rule is structurally simple even though the math behind it is not: next = any topic in the outer fringe (prerequisites met, not yet learned). The student picks among the ready topics; ALEKS doesn't force one order. For Blueberry, a hand-built prerequisite DAG over OChem topics (e.g., "nucleophile/electrophile" → "SN2" → "SN2 vs E2") gives about 80% of the benefit. Full KST state-space estimation isn't needed.
- The long diagnostic is a known friction point. ALEKS caps it at under 30 questions; Math Academy's is 30–45 minutes. A consumer app probably needs a much shorter optional placement, or placement inferred from the first sessions.
- The "I don't know" button is an honesty mechanism: it keeps guesses from corrupting the estimated state. Its downside (under-placement, making students re-learn known material) is a source of resentment.

### Gaps
- Could not read the full Initial Knowledge Check page to confirm exact 2025–2026 length (some institutions report 20–30 questions, but no primary current source was fetched).
- No public, current info on how ALEKS's organic chemistry knowledge space is structured (number of topics, prerequisite depth).

## 2. The pie chart and topic-mastery visualizations: why they motivate and how they're rendered

### Takeaway
The ALEKS Pie is a donut/pie split into slices by content area (chapters/categories). Each slice fills radially: a darker shade for mastered topics, a lighter shade for learned-but-not-yet-mastered topics, and empty space for what remains. Clicking a slice shows counts and the "ready to learn" topics. The unfilled space is a constant, visible completion gap, which is the core motivator. The two-tone learned vs. mastered encoding also makes a knowledge-check regression visible.

### Cited Findings
- "The darker color in the slice represents topics mastered, the lighter color represents topics learned, and the outer space without color represents the topics remaining to be learned and mastered." — [McGraw Hill, What is the ALEKS Pie?](https://www.mheducation.com/support/aleks-support-center/knowledge/what-is-the-aleks-pie.html)
- Each pie slice has three categories: Learned, Mastered, and Remaining. Selecting a slice shows real-time progress, and the legend on the right "updates with the slice name and the number of topics mastered, learned, and remaining" for that slice. — [ALEKS Knowledge Checks Overview for Students (aleks.com)](https://www.aleks.com/resources/ALEKS_Knowledge_Checks_Overview_for_Students.pdf); [McGraw Hill, What is the ALEKS Pie?](https://www.mheducation.com/support/aleks-support-center/knowledge/what-is-the-aleks-pie.html)
- After the Initial Knowledge Check, "the darker portion of each pie slice represents the topics that you have mastered and the lighter portion represents what you have yet to learn." (Older wording, from before the separate learned/mastered states.) — [ALEKS Student Module Reference Guide](https://www.aleks.com/highered/math/New_ALEKS_Student_Module_Reference_Guide.pdf)
- In ALEKS Adventure (the K–5 variant): "Solid color shading indicates what the student has mastered, and striped color shading indicates what the student has learned, but not yet mastered." — [McGraw Hill, Navigating the ALEKS Adventure Grade 3 Pie Chart](https://www.mheducation.com/prek-12/support/knowledge/aleks-adventure---grade-3-pie-chart.html)
- Instructors get the same visualization aggregated as a class-level "Pie Report." — [ALEKS Pie Report (Class Level)](https://www.aleks.com/highered/science/HE_Science_Pie_Report.pdf); [McGraw Hill, ALEKS Administrator Pie Reports](https://www.mheducation.com/prek-12/support/knowledge/aleks-admin-pie-report)
- Khan Academy's comparable view: on a unit page each skill shows as Not started / Attempted / Familiar / Proficient / Mastered, with each skill worth 100 Mastery Points, rolled up into unit and course mastery percentages. — [Khan Academy Help: How do Mastery levels work?](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/5548760867853--How-do-Khan-Academy-s-Mastery-levels-work); [Khan Academy Help: Course and Unit Mastery](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/115002552631-What-are-Course-and-Unit-Mastery)
- Brilliant's course path is a color-coded path of nodes and connecting lines built with Rive animations, paired with day streaks as "satisfying indicators and celebrations of their progress." — [Rive blog: How Brilliant.org motivates learners with animations](https://rive.app/blog/how-brilliant-org-motivates-learners-with-rive-animations)

### Inferences
- Why the pie compels: (a) a single glanceable "percent of the whole course" (a completion or Zeigarnik-style pull); (b) many small slices mean there's always a nearly-full slice to finish; (c) each learned topic moves the chart right away. It's also the gradebook, since instructors grade on pie percentage by a deadline (see Q5). That makes the drive extrinsic and coercive, not just intrinsic.
- Rendering recipe for Blueberry: slices = OChem units (e.g., bonding/resonance, acids-bases, stereochemistry, SN1/SN2/E1/E2, alkenes, carbonyls). Radial fill = topics completed / total. Two tones = "learned (fresh)" vs. "retained (passed a later review)." Tap a slice for a list of ready-to-learn topics. A striped pattern for "learned, not yet retained" (the Adventure approach) is more accessible than two shades of one hue.
- Keeping "learned" and "mastered" as separate visual states is a useful honesty device. Losing ground can show as mastered fading back to learned, not as progress vanishing.

### Gaps
- No verified source for the exact slice colors in Higher-Ed ALEKS (each slice is a distinct hue, but this could not be confirmed from a fetched page), or whether the 2025–2026 interface still uses the pie as the home view.
- No controlled study was found that isolates the pie visualization's effect on engagement.

## 3. The in-topic learning loop: mastery criteria, misses, explanations

### Takeaway
In Learning Mode, each topic has a progress bar that fills at 5 points. A correct answer earns +1, a second consecutive correct answer without viewing the Explanation earns +2, and a wrong answer costs −1. A fluent student therefore finishes a topic in about 3 correct answers in a row (1 + 2 + 2). Misses route to a worked explanation and a fresh algorithmic instance. "Learned" is still not "Mastered": mastery requires later confirmation in a Knowledge Check.

### Cited Findings
- "Students are awarded one point for a correct answer, and two points for two correct answers in a row without using the Explanation page. One point is subtracted for an incorrect answer. Once five points are earned within the topic, the progress indicator will be filled in and the topic will be learned." — [ALEKS Knowledge Checks Overview / Student guides (search snippet)](https://www.aleks.com/resources/ALEKS_Knowledge_Checks_Overview_for_Students.pdf); [ALEKS Student Module Reference Guide](https://www.aleks.com/highered/math/New_ALEKS_Student_Module_Reference_Guide.pdf)
- A course instruction page puts it more simply: "You complete a topic by clicking on a topic and completing three questions correctly in a row." (Consistent with the 1+2+2 arithmetic, but likely a simplification or an older rule.) — [UCSB ALEKS Instructions](https://people.chem.ucsb.edu/feldwinn/darby/ALEKS.htm)
- Mastery is a two-step process. Step 1: learn the topic ("ALEKS will give you credit for learning a topic when you answer several practice problems correctly (doesn't have to be in a row)"). Step 2: prove mastery. "Topics learned in Learning Mode are not considered mastered until retention is demonstrated in a Knowledge Check." — [ALEKS, All About Knowledge Checks (student overview)](https://www.aleks.com/resources/ALEKS_Knowledge_Checks_Overview_for_Students.pdf)
- "After two missed attempts at the same problem, the 'Continue' button takes the student to a new instance of the problem." — [ALEKS PPL Learning Module User Manual (UCSC)](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.ucsc.edu/dist/f/450/files/2017/03/ALEKS-PPL-Learning-Module-User-Manual.pdf?bid=450)
- ALEKS uses "algorithmically-generated questions" and interactive tools that go "beyond multiple choice by asking students to demonstrate chemical concepts" (e.g., drawing structures). — [ALEKS Course Products: Organic Chemistry 1](https://www.aleks.com/highered/science/course_products?cmscache=detailed&detailed=gscience13_ochema)
- Comparison: Khan Academy's skill-level ladder is Familiar (50 pts, 70%+ in practice or correct on a quiz/unit test), then Proficient (80 pts, correct again on a quiz/unit test or 100% on a quiz), then Mastered (100 pts, correct on the Unit test while Proficient). Levels can go down on quiz/test misses. — [Khan Academy Help: How do Mastery levels work?](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/5548760867853--How-do-Khan-Academy-s-Mastery-levels-work)
- Math Academy: every task (lesson, review, multistep, quiz) earns XP scaled by performance. Wrong answers penalize or adjust related concepts in the knowledge graph, and a community analysis reports that quizzes scored below roughly 43–58% can carry negative XP. — [Math Academy, How it works](https://www.mathacademy.com/how-it-works); [trilogy-group GitHub issue on MA pass rules (third-party, unofficial)](https://github.com/trilogy-group/mathacademy-timeback-skill/issues/28); [dgore review (Substack)](https://drgore.substack.com/p/my-review-of-math-academy)

### Inferences
- The +1/+2/−1 to 5 scheme is a cheap, transparent mastery rule for Blueberry. It rewards consecutive correct answers without requiring a strict streak, so one slip doesn't reset to zero, just −1. The "+2 only if you didn't open the explanation" clause discourages leaning on the explanation. Worth copying almost verbatim, maybe with a visible 5-segment bar.
- A known frustration: a wrong answer drops the bar, and repeated −1s on a hard OChem mechanism topic can feel like treadmill work. Blueberry could cap the penalty (never below the last checkpoint), or offer "switch topic" after 2 misses in a row. ALEKS itself hands over a fresh instance after 2 misses.
- The learned vs. mastered split is the key retention idea: first success is provisional until a delayed recheck confirms it.

### Gaps
- Could not verify whether ALEKS in 2025–2026 still uses exactly the 5-point bar in chemistry courses, or whether instructors can change the threshold.
- No primary source found on what the Explanation page shows for OChem mechanism items (for example, stepwise arrow-pushing).

## 4. Knowledge Checks: periodic reassessment that can remove topics, plus instructor/course structure

### Takeaway
Besides the initial assessment, ALEKS gives Progress Knowledge Checks (about 20 questions, mostly recently learned topics) automatically after a few hours of work and a threshold of learned topics, or at the end of instructor-set Objectives, plus Comprehensive checks (about 25 questions, the whole course). Topics missed in a check drop out of the "mastered/learned" part of the pie and must be re-learned. This is the mechanism students hate most. Instructors structure courses with Objectives and deadlines, and can grade on pie percentage or on Knowledge Check results against a "Goal."

### Cited Findings
- "Every course starts with an Initial Knowledge Check. Most courses will have Progress Knowledge Checks automatically appear after you've spent a few hours in the system and learned a certain number of topics." If the course has Objectives, "you may get a Knowledge Check after you complete each one." — [ALEKS, All About Knowledge Checks](https://www.aleks.com/resources/ALEKS_Knowledge_Checks_Overview_for_Students.pdf)
- "Progress Knowledge Checks consist of approximately 20 questions and will assess your students on material relevant to their individual learning histories (primarily topics recently learned). Comprehensive Knowledge Checks consist of approximately 25 questions and will assess your students on all the material in the class." — [ALEKS Instructor's Manual](https://www.aleks.com/manual/pdf/educators-hssl.pdf)
- Grading on a Knowledge Check uses a "Goal." For example, if midway through the class the goal is 50%, a student assessed as knowing 40% of the whole class scores 80% on that check. — [ALEKS Instructor's Manual / Student Module Reference Guide (search snippet)](https://www.aleks.com/manual/pdf/educators-hssl.pdf)
- Instructors can schedule Knowledge Checks manually. — [McGraw Hill, Schedule a New ALEKS Knowledge Check](https://www.mheducation.com/prek-12/support/knowledge/schedule-new-aleks-knowledge-check.html)
- A third-party guide (a commercial "do my ALEKS" service; low reliability, possible incentive to exaggerate) says Knowledge Checks "can appear with little or no warning, especially after Progress triggers (15-20 topics mastered)," and that they "can reset mastered topics back to 'learned' or 'remaining' status." — [Finish My Math Class, Complete Guide to ALEKS Knowledge Checks](https://finishmymathclass.com/complete-guide-to-aleks-knowledge-checks/); [Finish My Math Class, I Hate ALEKS](https://finishmymathclass.com/i-hate-aleks-reddit-complaints/)
- An IU South Bend chemistry placement page notes that periodic partial Knowledge Checks are "automatically offered after a sufficient number of topics have been learned and cover only those topics recently learned." — [IU South Bend: Chemistry Placement Exam and ALEKS Chemistry](https://southbend.iu.edu/students/placement/aleks-chem-exam.html)
- If instructors remove topics from a course, students no longer see them, and McGraw Hill warns this can remove prerequisites students need. — [McGraw Hill: What happens to the ALEKS Pie if topics are removed from my course?](https://www.mheducation.com/support/aleks-support-center/knowledge/what-happens-to-the-aleks-pie-if-topics-are-removed-from-my-course)
- Real syllabi show ALEKS used in organic/biochem courses (e.g., University of Florida CHM3217, 2020–2023). — [UF CHM3217 syllabus 2023](https://www.chem.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/89/2023/01/CHM3217-OrganicBiochem-1-Davidson.pdf)
- Khan Academy's analog: Mastery Challenges, always 6 questions covering 3 skills, unlock when a student has Familiar on 3+ skills, Proficient on 1+, and 12+ hours have passed since the last one. They can raise skills to Proficient/Mastered. Levels go down only on quiz/unit-test misses. — [Khan Academy Help: What are Mastery Challenges?](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/360037494231-What-are-Mastery-Challenges); [Khan Academy Help: How do Mastery levels work?](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/5548760867853--How-do-Khan-Academy-s-Mastery-levels-work)
- ALEKS research has found that retention of learned items changes as a function of time since the item was learned, which is the empirical basis for spaced rechecks. — [Matayoshi research summary (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/scientific-contributions/Jeffrey-Matayoshi-2132342267)
- Math Academy's spaced review uses "Fractional Implicit Repetition (FIRe)": reps on advanced topics "trickle down" the knowledge graph to update the review schedules of prerequisite topics, so practicing an advanced skill counts as partial review of simpler ones. — [Skycak, Optimized, Individualized Spaced Repetition in Hierarchical Knowledge Structures](https://www.justinmath.com/individualized-spaced-repetition-in-hierarchical-knowledge-structures/)

### Inferences
- The Knowledge Check is pedagogically sound (delayed retrieval practice, and it confirms mastery) but badly framed. It arrives unannounced, it's high-stakes (grades hinge on pie %), and failures are shown as visible loss. That turns "spaced review" into "the app took my progress."
- For Blueberry: keep spaced rechecks, but (a) make them short (3–6 items, like Khan's 6-question challenge), (b) announce them and make them opt-in or schedule them visibly, (c) show "needs review" decay (a fading or cracked tile) instead of deleting progress, and (d) consider FIRe-style implicit credit. For example, a correct E2 mechanism answer quietly refreshes "base strength" and "leaving groups," which cuts the review burden that makes ALEKS feel like a grind.

### Gaps
- No official, current source gives the exact trigger (topic count or hours) for Progress Knowledge Checks. The "15–20 topics" figure comes only from a low-reliability commercial site.
- Could not confirm from an official page exactly how failed topics are shown after a check (moved to "remaining" vs. "learned"), beyond the official learned/mastered framework.

## 5. Evidence of efficacy (ALEKS in chemistry) and student sentiment

### Takeaway
Quasi-experimental studies in general chemistry consistently find ALEKS improves grades, pass rates, and retention relative to traditional online homework, especially for weaker students. The same studies find students like it less and see it as more time-consuming. The core tension: effective but disliked. Student complaints focus on Knowledge Checks erasing progress, time burden, and explanations that don't match problems. No peer-reviewed ALEKS efficacy study specific to organic chemistry was found.

### Cited Findings
- Richards-Babb et al., J. Chem. Educ. 2018, 95(5), 691: a propensity-score-matched quasi-experimental study (n = 6,114 pairs) compared adaptive-responsive (ALEKS) with traditional-responsive online homework in general chemistry. The adaptive system "was found to increase the odds of a higher final letter grade for average, below average, and failing students." Yet students rated the adaptive system less favorably (3.15/5 vs. 3.31/5). — [ERIC EJ1178678](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1178678); [J. Chem. Educ. abstract](https://pubs.acs.org/jceda8/article-abstract/95/5/691/753202/General-Chemistry-Student-Attitudes-and-Success?redirectedFrom=fulltext)
- McGraw Hill's marketing, citing the West Virginia University implementation: CHEM 101 pass rate rose from 60.8% (fall 2009) to 73.9% (fall 2012) and average GPA from 1.96 to 2.42 after ALEKS adoption plus inter-departmental coordination of assignments and deadlines (a confounded, vendor-reported before/after). — [McGraw Hill ALEKS Chemistry Outcomes](https://www.mheducation.co.uk/higher-education/learning-solutions/aleks/chemistry-outcomes); [aleks.com science efficacy](https://aleks.com/highered/science/efficacy)
- Vendor-reported: Indiana University Bloomington compared 2,561 on-ground intro/prep chemistry students with 607 ALEKS Prep students and found ALEKS Prep "equally as effective," saving students about $1.5 million. A UC Davis study found students in an online ALEKS summer prep course reported higher motivation and STEM identity than those in a traditional course. — [McGraw Hill ALEKS Chemistry Outcomes (Canada)](https://www.mheducation.ca/higher-education/learning-solutions/aleks/chemistry-outcomes)
- Eichler & Peeples, J. Chem. Educ. 2013, 90(9), 1137–1143, "Online Homework Put to the Test," compared two online learning systems (ALEKS vs. a traditional system) in general chemistry. Students completing a precourse ALEKS assignment could expect final exam scores over 13 points higher than non-participants, vs. about 8 points for a MasteringChemistry precourse assignment. — [ACS: Eichler & Peeples 2013](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/ed3006264); [snippet via Springer JOST 2021 article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10956-020-09872-2)
- J. Sci. Educ. Technol. (2021): in two gen-chem sections (ALEKS vs. Sapling), ALEKS students "scored significantly higher on the exams and retained significantly more information." Students saw ALEKS as better exam preparation but "complained that the system was more time-consuming." — [Springer, JOST 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10956-020-09872-2); [ERIC EJ1280508](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1280508)
- There is a related 2023 JOST study on students' perceptions of online homework systems in general chemistry, and a meta-analysis of ALEKS effects on mathematics learning (K–12 and higher ed). Details not retrieved. — [Springer JOST 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10956-023-10061-0); [ALEKS math meta-analysis (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/352743853_The_Effects_of_ALEKS_on_Mathematics_Learning_in_K-12_and_Higher_Education_A_Meta-Analysis)
- Student-complaint aggregation (commercial homework-help site summarizing Reddit; potential bias): recurring complaints are "Knowledge Checks that erase hours of progress, explanations that don't match problems, lockout systems that prevent homework completion, and overwhelming time requirements," and some students call ALEKS the hardest part of their chemistry course. — [Finish My Math Class: I Hate ALEKS – Reddit complaints](https://finishmymathclass.com/i-hate-aleks-reddit-complaints/)
- Aggregated reviews also exist on Trustpilot and G2. Content not retrieved. — [Trustpilot: aleks.com](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/aleks.com); [G2: ALEKS reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/aleks/reviews)

### Inferences
- The efficacy and dislike findings come from the same mechanisms: adaptive gating plus periodic reassessment force retrieval practice and prevent skipping. Those same features create lost-progress pain and time costs. Richards-Babb's 3.15 vs. 3.31 is a modest attitude gap, much smaller than the loud online sentiment suggests, but it points consistently the same way.
- "Compelled" = the pie is tied to the grade and the deadline (extrinsic), plus the completion gap. "Dislike" = no autonomy (locked topics, forced checks), perceived punishment (−1 points, lost topics), and unpredictability (surprise checks). A voluntary study app lacks the grade lever, so it can't afford ALEKS-level punishment. Students will simply leave.
- Most of the evidence is from general chemistry and is quasi-experimental or vendor-reported. It's reasonable to generalize the retrieval/mastery mechanics to OChem, but that isn't directly evidenced.

### Gaps
- Direct Reddit threads (r/chemistry, r/OrganicChemistry, r/college) and RateMyProfessors comments were not retrieved (fetch blocked). The sentiment evidence above is second-hand via a commercial aggregator.
- No peer-reviewed study of ALEKS Organic Chemistry 1/2 outcomes was found.
- The Eichler & Peeples "13 vs. 8 points" figure came from a search snippet; which system was compared (MasteringChemistry) should be checked against the full paper.

## 6. What a gamified app could borrow or avoid, and other mastery systems (Khan Academy, Math Academy, Brilliant)

### Takeaway
Borrow ALEKS's prerequisite graph with "ready to learn" gating, the transparent points-to-learn bar, the learned vs. mastered distinction, and short spaced rechecks. Avoid unannounced high-stakes checks, visible deletion of progress, locked-down autonomy, and long diagnostics. Khan Academy shows a gentler multi-level ladder. Math Academy shows graph-aware spaced repetition with effort-based XP. Brilliant shows how much motivation comes from an animated path, streaks, and weekly leagues.

### Cited Findings
- Khan Academy: 5 visible skill states (Not started, Attempted, Familiar, Proficient, Mastered); 100 Mastery Points per skill (Familiar 50, Proficient 80, Mastered 100); levels can go up or down based on quizzes and unit tests; Mastery Challenges are 6 questions over 3 skills with a 12-hour cooldown. — [Khan Academy Help: Mastery levels](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/5548760867853--How-do-Khan-Academy-s-Mastery-levels-work); [Khan Academy Help: Mastery Challenges](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/360037494231-What-are-Mastery-Challenges)
- Math Academy: a knowledge graph spanning roughly 4th-grade through university math; an adaptive diagnostic (about 30–45 min) sets the "knowledge frontier"; FIRe spaced repetition propagates credit and penalties through the graph; 1 XP ≈ 1 minute of focused effort; leagues and "on track" tools for accountability. — [Skycak, Tip of Math Academy's Technical Iceberg](https://www.justinmath.com/the-tip-of-math-academys-technical-iceberg/); [Skycak, FIRe](https://www.justinmath.com/individualized-spaced-repetition-in-hierarchical-knowledge-structures/); [Math Academy, How our AI works](https://www.mathacademy.com/how-our-ai-works); [dgore review](https://drgore.substack.com/p/my-review-of-math-academy)
- Math Academy XP depends on performance, and low-scoring quizzes/multisteps can carry negative XP, according to a third-party analysis. — [trilogy-group GitHub issue (unofficial)](https://github.com/trilogy-group/mathacademy-timeback-skill/issues/28)
- Independent reviewers discuss Math Academy's product features and pedagogy tradeoffs at length (e.g., frankhecker.com's multi-part series, Oz Nova's "balanced review"). — [frankhecker.com Math Academy part 5: Product features](https://frankhecker.com/2025/02/12/math-academy-part-5/); [Oz Nova, A balanced review of Math Academy](https://newsletter.ozwrites.com/p/a-balanced-review-of-math-academy)
- Brilliant: weekly Leagues reset Sundays at 8 PM PT across 10 tiers named after elements (Hydrogen → Lithium → Carbon → Neon → Titanium → Xenon → Barium → Neodymium → Tungsten → Einsteinium), with promotion or demotion by weekly XP rank; streak = consecutive learning days; XP for completing lessons and solving problems. — [Brilliant Help: Leagues and leaderboards](https://help.brilliant.org/en/articles/3141056); [Brilliant Help: What is XP?](https://brilliant.org/help/features/what-is-xp/)
- Brilliant's learning path nodes and connecting lines are built with Rive, and paths are color-coded by topic. — [Rive blog on Brilliant](https://rive.app/blog/how-brilliant-org-motivates-learners-with-rive-animations)

### Inferences
Borrow list for Blueberry (OChem):
1. **Prerequisite graph with "Ready to Learn" frontier**: show 3–6 unlockable topics at a time (ALEKS outer fringe, Math Academy frontier), with the student choosing among them for autonomy.
2. **Mastery pie or map** by unit, two-tone (learned vs. retained) plus a remaining ring. Consider Brilliant-style animated path nodes inside each unit. Chemistry-themed flourishes fit, as Brilliant's element-named leagues show.
3. **5-point topic bar** (+1 correct, +2 for a consecutive correct without hints, −1 on a miss, floored so it never drops below 0 or the last checkpoint).
4. **Short, announced spaced rechecks** (Khan-style 6 questions with a cooldown), with FIRe-style implicit credit so advanced-mechanism practice refreshes prerequisites.
5. **Effort-honest XP** (Math Academy: XP ≈ minutes of focused work) instead of XP for taps, to resist gaming.
6. **Optional short placement**, or "test out of this unit," instead of a mandatory ~30-question diagnostic.

Avoid list:
1. Surprise, high-stakes knowledge checks that wipe visible progress. Use decay ("needs review") and one-tap recovery instead.
2. Hard lockouts with no escape hatch. Allow previewing locked topics or a "try anyway" option.
3. Negative XP or visible point loss that feels punitive in a voluntary app (Math Academy and ALEKS can get away with it because of paid or graded commitment).
4. Generic explanations that don't match the exact item (a top ALEKS complaint). For OChem, explanations should reference the specific substrate and mechanism step.

### Gaps
- Khan Academy's newest (2024–2026, Khanmigo-era) mastery changes weren't checked. The help-center pages cited may predate changes.
- Brilliant's current (2025–2026) path/level structure and any mastery-based review features weren't confirmed beyond the help-center pages.
- No public data comparing retention or engagement between ALEKS-style pies, Khan-style skill grids, and Brilliant-style paths.
