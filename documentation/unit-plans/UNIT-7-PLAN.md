### u7-mechanism: The carbonyl and the addition step
- Stage / kind / role: A / spine / video (*kept* first; plays reaction `carbonyl-addition` as its
  trainer step)
- Teaches: u7:carbonyl-polarity, u7:addition-alkoxide, u7:workup, u7:reactivity-order
- Assumes: u0:polar-bond (NEW), u0:orbitals, u0:hybridization (planned), u2:induction
- Primer:
  - Big question: "Every reagent in this unit goes to the same carbon. Which one, and why?"
  - Pretest: `u7-mech-alkoxide` (guess before teaching).
  - Ideas:
    1. O takes more of the C=O electrons, so C is partly positive and O partly negative.
    2. A nucleophile bonds to that C, and the π pair moves onto O, giving an alkoxide. The carbon
       goes from flat (sp2) to tetrahedral (sp3).
    3. Workup is its own step: H3O⁺ puts H on the O⁻.
    4. Aldehydes are more reactive than ketones, because they have one fewer alkyl donor and less
       crowding. This is a course convention, which the script states.
    5. A flat carbon can be reached from either face, a preview of u7-faces.
- Worked example (**NEW**):
  - Problem: cyclopentanone `O=C1CCCC1` with CH3MgBr, then H3O⁺.
  - Steps:
    1. Find the C of C=O (δ+).
    2. CH3 bonds to it and the π pair goes to O, giving 1-methylcyclopentan-1-olate
       `CC1([O-])CCCC1`.
    3. A separate H3O⁺ step gives 1-methylcyclopentan-1-ol `CC1(O)CCCC1`.
  - Round-1 check: rule A gives that product (`check_plan_smiles.py`).
  - Pool check: cyclopentanone is in the generator's species table, but only in u7-bv and u7-mpv.
    No u7-mechanism question draws it.
  - Figure: skeletal structures with the curved arrows from the trainer's arrow set, plus the
    alkoxide with its charge drawn.
- First step (fade line): "First step: find the carbonyl carbon. The nucleophile bonds there and
  the C=O pair moves onto O."

### u7-grignard: Grignard, organolithium and acetylide addition
- Stage / kind / role: A / spine / practice (*reordered* from seventh to second; plays
  `grignard-methyl`)
- Teaches: u7:grignard-prep, u7:grignard-alcohol-class, u7:carbonation, u7:acetylide (NEW),
  u7:grignard-protic, u7:grignard-retro
- Assumes: u7:addition-alkoxide, u7:workup, u2:keq-pka, u0:conjugate-base (planned)
- Primer:
  - Big question: "A Grignard is a carbon that acts like an anion. What does it build, and what
    kills it?"
  - Pretest: `u7-grg-ket`.
  - Ideas:
    1. R-Br with Mg in dry ether gives R-MgBr, a carbon nucleophile (a carbanion equivalent).
    2. Methanal gives a 1° alcohol, any other aldehyde a 2°, a ketone a 3°. Count the carbons on
       the carbonyl carbon.
    3. CO2 gives a carboxylic acid with one carbon more.
    4. An acetylide (from a terminal alkyne, pKa 25 on the ladder) adds the same way.
    5. Any O-H or N-H in the flask destroys it first. The R-H it makes has pKa about 50 against 16
       for an alcohol, so the proton transfer is effectively complete.
- Worked example (**NEW**):
  - Problem: pentan-3-one `CCC(=O)CC` with C6H5MgBr, then H3O⁺.
  - Steps:
    1. The carbonyl carbon carries two carbons, so the product will be a 3° alcohol.
    2. Phenyl bonds to C and O⁻ forms.
    3. Workup gives 3-phenylpentan-3-ol `CCC(O)(CC)c1ccccc1` (derived, rule A).
    4. Backwards: cut either C-C bond at the carbinol carbon. Phenyl plus pentan-3-one, or ethyl
       plus 1-phenylpropan-1-one, makes the same alcohol.
  - Pool check: pentan-3-one is in the pool only in u7-hydration, u7-bv and u7-mpv.
  - Figure: skeletal, with the two disconnections coloured.
- First step (fade line): "First step: count the carbons on the carbonyl carbon. The Grignard adds
  one more, so methanal, aldehyde and ketone give 1°, 2° and 3°."
- Practice, in play order:
  - u7-grg-meet mcq L0
  - u7-grg-mg mcq L1

  - u7-grg-co2 mcq L1

    pent-1-yn-3-ol `C#CC(O)CC`. The species is not in the pool.
  - u7-grg-proton mcq L2
  - **NEW u7-grg-why-proton** mcq L2
  - u7-grg-retro mcq L2
  - u7-grg-match match L2 (*retagged* from `[1, 2]`)
  - trainer `grignard-methyl`
  - **NEW u7-grg-draw** draw L3: cyclopentanone with C6H5MgBr, then H3O⁺. The claim is
