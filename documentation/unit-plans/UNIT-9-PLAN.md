## Lessons

### u9-tautomer: Keto and enol, the same molecule twice
- Stage / kind / role: A / spine / video (*kept* first; plays `seq-tautomer`)
- Teaches: u9:tautomer, u9:alpha-carbon, u9:keto-favoured, u9:acid-base-catalysis-enol
- Assumes: u0:resonance, u0:equilibrium, u7:enol-to-keto, u7:reactivity-order
- Primer:
  - Big question: "Why does a ketone sometimes act as if it had a C=C?"
  - Pretest: `u9-tau-meet` (guess before teaching).
  - Ideas:
    1. An α-carbon is the sp3 carbon next to C=O; its H's are α-H.
    2. Moving one α-H to O, with the π bond shifting, makes the enol. The two are tautomers:
       different atoms connected, not resonance forms.
    3. The keto form wins the equilibrium for simple ketones (C=O is the stronger π bond); a
       trace of the enol is always there.
    4. Acid or base speeds the swap; neither changes where it ends.
    5. A molecule with no α-H has no enol.
- Worked example (**NEW**):
  - Problem: cyclopentanone `O=C1CCCC1`, a trace of acid. Draw its enol.
  - Steps:
    1. Find the α-carbons (both ring CH2 next to C=O; symmetry makes one enol).
    2. Protonate the carbonyl O ("acid first goes to the most basic site").
    3. Remove an α-H with water; the electrons become the C=C: cyclopent-1-en-1-ol `OC1=CCCC1`.
  - Derived round 1 by rule E (`check_plan_u9.py`). Cyclopentanone is a literal only in the
    dieckmann, intra and robinson blocks; no u9-tautomer item draws it.
  - Figure: keto and enol skeletal, with the two-step acid arrows from the trainer's arrow set.
- First step (fade line): "First step: find the α-carbon and the H on it. That H moves to the
  oxygen."
- Practice, in play order:
