## Lessons

### u10-basicity: The N lone pair, and how basic it is
- Stage / kind / role: A / spine / video (*kept* first; plays beat `u10-basicity`, the pool's amine
  sort)
- Teaches: u10:lone-pair-class, u10:basicity-order, u10:pkah, u10:amine-salt
- Assumes: u0:resonance, u0:conjugate-base (planned), u0:nuc-vs-base (NEW), u2:keq-pka,
  u7:amine-class, u8:amide-resonance, u8:acid-amine-salt
- Primer:
  - Big question: "Every amine has a lone pair on N. Why does one grab a proton eagerly and another
    barely at all?"
  - Pretest: `u10-bas-meet`.
  - Ideas:
    1. An amine is a base because of the lone pair on N; the salt it makes is R-NH₃⁺, pKa about 10
       on the course ladder. That is the number for the salt, not for the amine's own N-H (35).
    2. When only alkyl groups sit on N, the lone pair stays put: the strongest base in the unit.
    3. A ring on N spreads the lone pair into the ring by resonance (aniline): weaker.
    4. A C=O on N shares the lone pair with the oxygen (an amide): barely a base at all.
    5. Rank by asking where the lone pair goes, never by counting H's on N.
- Worked example (**NEW**):
  - Problem: rank benzylamine `NCc1ccccc1`, N-methylaniline `CNc1ccccc1` and N-methylacetamide
    `CNC(C)=O` as bases.
  - Steps:
    1. Benzylamine: the ring is one carbon away, so it cannot reach the lone pair. Alkyl N,
       strongest ("the CH₂ insulates the N").
    2. N-methylaniline: N sits on the ring, so its lone pair is shared into it. Aryl N, middle.
    3. N-methylacetamide: N sits on a C=O, so its lone pair is shared with O. Amide N, weakest.
  - Derived round 1 by rule L (`check_plan_u10.py`: classes alkyl, aryl, amide). None of the three
    is a literal in the u10-basicity block. The order is BASIC_CONV, the outline's convention.
  - Figure: the three skeletal structures, the lone pair drawn on N, with the aniline's one
    ring-sharing contributor (8e resonance row) and the amide's C=O contributor.
- First step (fade line): "First step: look at what is bonded to the N. Alkyl only, a ring, or a
  C=O?"
- Practice, in play order:
  - u10-bas-meet mcq L0
  - u10-bas-weakest mcq L1
