## Lessons

### u6-dou: Degrees of unsaturation
- Stage / kind / role: A / spine / video (*kept* first)
- Teaches: u6:dou, u6:dou-heteroatoms, u6:aryl-hint
- Assumes: u0:orbitals, u0:functional-groups (NEW)
- Primer: big question "Before any spectrum, what can the formula alone rule out?"; pretest
  `u6-dou-fit-c8h10`; ideas: (1) a saturated, open-chain compound holds 2C + 2 H; (2) each ring or
  pi bond costs two H, so (2C + 2 − H) / 2 counts rings plus pi bonds; (3) a halogen counts as an H,
  N adds one, O changes nothing; (4) four or more suggests a benzene ring (one ring plus three pi
  bonds), the instructor's first step.
- Worked example: **NEW**. Benzyl alcohol `OCc1ccccc1`, C7H8O. Steps: (1) ignore the O, it adds
  no H demand; (2) saturated H for seven carbons is 2(7) + 2 = 16; (3) 16 − 8 = 8 missing, so 4
  degrees; (4) 4 means suspect a benzene ring first. Derived: DoU 4 from the formula and from the
  structure (rule 1, the two must agree). Species not in the pool. Figure: skeletal structure, the
  ring and the three ring pi bonds highlighted, "1 ring + 3 π = 4".
- First step (fade line): "First step: find the H a saturated, open-chain compound with these
  carbons would hold, 2C + 2, counting each halogen as an H."
- Practice (play order): u6-dou-meet-ring mcq L0; u6-dou-c6h10 mcq L1; u6-dou-halogen mcq L1
  (feedback text rewritten per audit row 112, *kept*); **NEW u6-dou-why-halogen** mcq L1;
  u6-dou-ester mcq L1; u6-dou-fit-c3h6o mcq L1; u6-dou-fit-c8h10 mcq L1; **NEW u6-dou-why-four**
  mcq L2; u6-dou-nitrogen mcq L2; u6-dou-not-c6h12 mcq L2; u6-exam-match-dou match L2 (*moved*
  from `U6_UNWIRED` into this lesson as its connect step); **NEW u6-dou-draw-ring** draw L3 ("C3H6,
  no C=C. Draw it." Key cyclopropane `C1CC1`, derived DoU 1; the only other C3H6 is propene
  `C=CC`, so the key is unique, claim to check by rule 1 on both).
- Why items: u6-dou-why-halogen ("Why does Br count as an H? It makes one bond, where an H would
  have been") pairs with u6-dou-halogen; u6-dou-why-four ("Why does 4 point to a benzene ring? One
  ring and three pi bonds use exactly four") pairs with u6-dou-fit-c8h10.
- Say-why mix-ups:
  1. "Oxygen takes away H, so it raises the count." Fix: O makes two bonds and sits in a chain
     without changing how many H the carbons can hold; leave it out.
  2. "Any 4 means benzene." Fix: 4 suggests a ring first; a ring plus three C=C or C≡C can give 4
     too (u6-dou-not-c6h12 confronts "one degree must be C=C").
- Picture: "Any molecule" row: skeletal structures, one ring or pi bond circled per degree.
- Video slot: 4 minutes; shows: the 2C + 2 count on a straight chain, built bond by bond; closing
  a ring and adding a pi bond each removing two H; the four-degree benzene ring.

### u6-ir: IR, the bonds the molecule holds
- Stage / kind / role: A / spine / practice (*kept*; plays its MCQs then `IR_SIGNAL_BOARD`)
- Teaches: u6:ir-carbonyl, u6:ir-oh-nh, u6:ir-3000-line, u6:ir-co-shift

  pretest `u6-ir-ketone`; ideas (bands *kept* as signed off 24 Oct, cited OpenStax 12.7, 12.8,
