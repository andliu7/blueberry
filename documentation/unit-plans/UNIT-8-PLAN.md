### u8-acidity: Why a carboxylic acid is an acid (NEW)
- Stage / kind / role: A / spine / video (**NEW**, opens the unit; no trainer; plays the authored
  `resonance` beat for acetate if one exists, else text plus figure)
- Teaches: u8:acid-pka, u8:carboxylate-resonance, u8:extraction-keq, u8:acid-amine-salt,
  u8:acid-kills-organometal
- Assumes: u0:conjugate-base (planned, Unit 2), u0:resonance, u0:equilibrium, u2:keq-pka,
  u0:functional-groups (planned)
- Exam weight: Act 2 pKa row, 7 points (numeric, "explain with structures and no words");
  `carboxylic_acids` extraction argued with Keq; F17 "draw the conjugate acid salt".
- Primer:
  - Big question: "Vinegar's acid and ethanol both have an O-H. Why does only one of them turn
    blue litmus red?"
  - Pretest: `u8-acd-meet` (guess before teaching).
  - Ideas:
    1. Judge an acid by the base it leaves behind, never by the O-H bond itself.
    2. A carboxylate shares its negative charge between two equal oxygens (draw both
       contributors); an alkoxide keeps it on one.
    3. An acid-base equilibrium sits on the side of the weaker acid. Course ladder: carboxylic
       acid 5, carbonic acid 6.4, phenol 10, alcohol 16.
    4. So NaHCO₃ pulls a carboxylic acid into water but leaves a phenol or an alcohol behind.
    5. An amine (ammonium 10) or a Grignard (alkane 50) takes the acid's proton before doing
       anything else.
- Worked example (**NEW**):
  - Problem: butanoic acid `CCCC(=O)O` and butan-1-ol `CCCCO` dissolved in ether, shaken with
    aqueous NaHCO₃. Which layer does each end in?
  - Steps:
    1. Find each acidic H: the CO₂H (about 5) and the O-H of the alcohol (about 16).
    2. Compare each with the base's conjugate acid, carbonic acid (6.4). The equilibrium lies
       toward the weaker acid: for butanoic acid that is the product side, giving butanoate
       `CCCC(=O)[O-]`; for butan-1-ol it is the starting side.
    3. Butanoate is a charged salt, so it goes into the water layer. Butan-1-ol stays in ether.
    4. To get the acid back, add H₃O⁺ to the water layer, as a separate step.
  - Round-1 check: deprotonation derived (`check_plan_smiles.py`); the two-sided comparison is a
    lookup on `pka.ts`, with no arithmetic shown.
  - Pool check: this is a new lesson; neither species is in its NEW items (below).
  - Figure: a two-layer funnel with skeletal structures, butanoate drawn as both contributors
    with curved arrows.
- First step (fade line): "First step: find each acidic H and its pKa, then compare it with the
  pKa of the base's conjugate acid."
- Practice, in play order:
  - **NEW u8-acd-meet** mcq L0: pick the most acidic H in propanoic acid `CCC(=O)O`.
  - **NEW u8-acd-rank** sort L1: benzoic acid, phenol, ethanol, most acidic first (course ladder
    values; a sort, not a list in the prompt).
  - **NEW u8-acd-why-res** mcq L1: why acetic acid is far more acidic than ethanol. Its
    conjugate base spreads the charge over two O (RDKit: acetate has 2 contributors with the
    charge on 2 O; ethoxide has 1). Pairs with u8-acd-rank.
  - u8-ste-salt mcq L1 (*moved* from u8-steglich): acetic acid with CH₃NH₂ at room temperature

### u8-tetrahedral: Add, then let the leaving group go
