## Lessons

### u5-roh-rx: Alcohol to alkyl halide
- Stage / kind / role: A / spine / video (*kept* first; plays `seq-roh-hbr` as its trainer step)
- Teaches: u5:oh-activation, u5:hx-route, u5:pbr3-socl2
- Assumes: u0:leaving-group (NEW), u0:subst-elim (NEW), u0:carbocation, u0:rearrangement
- Primer: big question "Hydroxide almost never leaves carbon. How do you make an alcohol's OH
  leave?"; pretest `u5-rx-tert` (guess before teaching); ideas: (1) OH⁻ leaves badly because its
  conjugate acid, water, is weak (ladder `water` 15.7); H2O leaves well because its conjugate acid,
  H3O⁺, is strong (ladder `hydronium` −1.7); (2) fix one: protonate it with HX, water leaves, a
  2° or 3° carbon becomes a cation and the cation can shift; (3) fix two: hang the O on P or S
  (PBr3, SOCl2), no cation, the halide comes in from behind, so inversion and no shift; (4) a 1°
  alcohol with HX has no cation to make, so the halide pushes water out from behind (outline: "HX
  substitution across the substrate ladder", course convention).
- Worked example: **NEW**. (S)-pentan-2-ol `CCC[C@H](C)O` meets PBr3. Steps: (1) the carbon is
  2°, so HBr would risk a cation; PBr3 never makes one; (2) the O attacks P, becoming a leaving
  group on phosphorus; (3) bromide attacks the back of that carbon as the O leaves, so the three
  other groups flip; (4) product (R)-2-bromopentane `CCC[C@@H](C)Br`. Round-1 check: the graph
  inversion gives S in, R out (`check_plan_smiles.py`); the generator must build it in 3D as rule S
  does. Species not in the pool (pool uses butan-2-ol, 3-methylbutan-2-ol; grep found no
  pentan-2-ol). Figure: turnable 3-D model of the backside attack, plus the two skeletal structures
  with wedges.
- First step (fade line): "First step: decide whether a cation can form. HX on a 2° or 3° carbon
  makes one; PBr3 and SOCl2 never do."
- Practice (play order): u5-rx-meet mcq L0; u5-rx-tert mcq L1; u5-rx-invert mcq L1;
  u5-rx-socl2 mcq L1; **NEW u5-rx-why-lg** mcq L1; u5-rx-shift mcq L2; **NEW u5-rx-why-shift**
  mcq L2; u5-rx-noshift mcq L2; u5-rx-which-shifts mcq L2; trainer `seq-roh-hbr`; **NEW
  u5-rx-draw** draw L3 (3-methylpentan-2-ol `CCC(C)C(C)O` with HBr: draw the major product;
  claim to derive by rule C: hydride shift to the 3° carbon, 3-bromo-3-methylpentane
  `CCC(C)(Br)CC`; species not in the pool).
- Why items: u5-rx-why-lg ("Why does HBr work on the OH when NaBr alone does not? Water leaves,
  hydroxide does not: compare their conjugate acids") pairs with u5-rx-tert; u5-rx-why-shift
  ("why does Br end up on C3, not C2: the cation moves to the carbon with more carbon
  neighbours") pairs with u5-rx-shift.
- Say-why mix-ups:
  1. "Heat an alcohol and the OH falls off on its own." Fix: hydroxide is a poor leaving group
     (its conjugate acid, water, is weak); acid or P/S has to change it first.
  2. "PBr3 rearranges just like HBr does." Fix: PBr3 makes no cation; bromide attacks from behind,
     so nothing shifts and the carbon inverts.
- Picture: 8e "Spatial: a turnable 3-D model" (the inversion umbrella); "Mechanism: the trainer"
  (`seq-roh-hbr`); text for the LG rule.
- Video slot: 3 minutes. Shows: H2O versus OH⁻ as leaving groups, read off their conjugate acids'
  pKa; HBr on a 2° alcohol making a cation that shifts; PBr3 on the same alcohol, backside attack,
  inversion, no shift.

### u5-tosylate: Tosylates and mesylates
- Stage / kind / role: A / spine / practice (plays `seq-mesylate` as its trainer step)
- Teaches: u5:sulfonate, u5:two-step-sn2
- Assumes: u5:oh-activation, u5:pbr3-socl2, u0:subst-elim, u0:resonance
- Primer: big question "How do you swap an OH for any nucleophile and still control the
  stereocentre?"; pretest `u5-ts-bond`; ideas: (1) TsCl reacts at the O, so the O-H breaks and the
  C-O stays: retention; (2) the tosylate leaves about as well as a halide, because its charge
  spreads over three O by resonance; (3) a nucleophile then does SN2: inversion; (4) retention
  then inversion is inversion overall; (5) pyridine or Et3N only takes the HCl.

  so the tosylate is still cis; (2) cyanide attacks that carbon from the face opposite the
