# Organic Chemistry II, Extensive Reagent Reference

The owner's reference list (Andrew, 2026-10-20). It is deliberately over-inclusive: some reagents and name reactions appear only in certain curricula.
Used to audit Blueberry's coverage (audit: `grignard/reports/curriculum-vs-reference-2026-10-20.md`, outside the repo).
Topics follow the common McMurry/Wade teaching order; Clayden and Carey reshuffle. Each topic lists reagents/conditions per transformation and the foundations to master first.

## 1. Conjugation, Resonance & Dienes

Often taught at the end of Orgo I rather than II; varies by school.

Reactions (reagents / conditions):
- 1,2- vs 1,4-addition to conjugated dienes: HX (HCl, HBr, HI); kinetic (1,2) at low T, about -80 °C; thermodynamic (1,4) at higher T, about +40 °C, or with a catalyst
- Addition of X2 (Br2, Cl2) to dienes gives 1,2- and 1,4-dihalides
- Diels-Alder: conjugated diene (s-cis) + dienophile (alkene/alkyne bearing an EWG: maleic anhydride, acrolein, acrylonitrile, benzoquinone, TCNE) gives a cyclohexene; heat; suprafacial-suprafacial, stereospecific, endo rule
- Inverse/hetero Diels-Alder (some courses): carbonyl or imine as dienophile/diene
- Allylic halogenation: NBS, hν or ROOR (radical initiator), CCl4
- Radical polymerization of dienes (mention): initiator, heat

Foundations: MO theory (HOMO/LUMO, FMO); resonance and allylic delocalization; Hammond postulate; kinetic vs thermodynamic control; carbocation/radical stability; R/S and E/Z stereochemistry.

## 2. Aromaticity & Benzene

Conceptual; sometimes closes Orgo I. Nomenclature review typically included.

Reactions (reagents / conditions):
- No new preparative reactions; a foundational chapter
- Recognizing aromatic / antiaromatic / nonaromatic systems; annulenes; heterocycles (pyridine, pyrrole, furan, thiophene); cyclopentadienyl anion; tropylium (cycloheptatrienyl) cation

Foundations: Hückel 4n+2 rule; cyclic continuous conjugation; planarity requirement; resonance energy; lone-pair participation vs non-participation; NMR ring-current anisotropy.

## 3. Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution (EAS)

Universally covered; the core of Orgo II.

Reactions (reagents / conditions):
- Halogenation: Cl2/FeCl3; Br2/FeBr3 (or Fe); I2 needs an oxidant (HNO3, CuCl2, H2O2); F rarely direct
- Nitration: HNO3/H2SO4 (electrophile NO2+); fuming HNO3 for dinitration
- Sulfonation: SO3/H2SO4 (fuming, oleum); reversible (dilute H2SO4, steam, heat removes it; used as a blocking group)
- Friedel-Crafts alkylation: R-X/AlCl3 (or FeCl3); also alkenes/H+ or ROH/H+; carbocation rearrangements occur; fails on strongly deactivated rings and on aryl-NH2
- Friedel-Crafts acylation: RCOCl/AlCl3, or (RCO)2O/AlCl3; no rearrangement; monosubstitution (the product is deactivated)
- Gattermann-Koch formylation (some courses): CO, HCl, AlCl3/CuCl gives ArCHO
- Gattermann / Houben-Hoesch, Vilsmeier (POCl3, DMF) formylations/acylations: advanced/optional
- Kolbe-Schmitt (optional): phenoxide + CO2, heat, pressure gives salicylic acid
- Reimer-Tiemann (optional): phenol, CHCl3, NaOH gives o-hydroxybenzaldehyde
- Side-chain reactions: benzylic bromination (NBS, hν); benzylic oxidation with KMnO4 or Na2Cr2O7/H+ (heat) gives ArCOOH (needs a benzylic H)
- Carbonyl to CH2 after F-C acylation: Clemmensen Zn(Hg)/HCl; Wolff-Kishner N2H4/KOH, heat; also H2/Pd or Et3SiH
- Nitro reduction: H2/Pd (or Pt, Ni); Fe/HCl; Sn/HCl; SnCl2; Zn/HCl gives Ar-NH2
- Birch reduction: Na (or Li, K) / NH3(l) / ROH gives a 1,4-cyclohexadiene (EWG rings reduce at ipso/para; EDG rings at ortho/meta)

Foundations: ortho/para vs meta directors; activators vs deactivators (resonance + inductive); arenium (σ-complex) intermediate; carbocation stability and rearrangement; blocking-group strategy; synthesis order/sequencing logic.

## 4. Nucleophilic Aromatic Substitution & Benzyne

Depth varies; the benzyne mechanism is sometimes omitted.

Reactions (reagents / conditions):
- SNAr (addition-elimination): aryl halide with a strong o/p EWG (especially NO2) + Nu- (OH-, OR-, NH2R, CN-, SR-); Meisenheimer intermediate; leaving-group rate F > Cl > Br > I (opposite of usual)
- Benzyne (elimination-addition): aryl halide + strong base NaNH2/NH3 (or KNH2, LDA, n-BuLi); no ortho EWG required; gives a mixture from symmetric addition
- Phenol synthesis via SNAr: chlorobenzene + NaOH, about 350 °C, high pressure (Dow process, historical mention)

Foundations: EAS directing logic (effects reversed here); anion resonance stabilization; leaving-group ability in each mechanism; elimination fundamentals; isotope labeling to prove mechanism.

## 5. Alcohols, Diols, Ethers & Epoxides

Alcohol preparation is often in Orgo I; the reaction chemistry frequently lands in II. Thiols/sulfides sometimes appended.

Reactions (reagents / conditions):
- Alcohol to alkyl halide: SOCl2 (+ pyridine); PBr3; PCl3/PCl5; P/I2 for RI; HX (HCl needs ZnCl2 for 1°/2°; 3° reacts readily); Appel: CBr4/PPh3
- Alcohol to tosylate/mesylate: TsCl / MsCl, pyridine (or Et3N) gives a good leaving group
- Dehydration: conc. H2SO4 or H3PO4, heat (E1, Zaitsev); POCl3/pyridine (E2, milder)
- Oxidation: PCC or PDC (1° to aldehyde); DMP (Dess-Martin); Swern (oxalyl chloride, DMSO, Et3N); TEMPO/NaOCl; Jones (CrO3/H2SO4), Na2Cr2O7, KMnO4 (1° to COOH, 2° to ketone); 3° not oxidized
- Williamson ether synthesis: R-O- (from NaH or Na) + 1° R'-X (SN2)
- Alkoxymercuration-demercuration: Hg(OAc)2/ROH then NaBH4 gives an ether (Markovnikov, no rearrangement)
- Ether cleavage: HI or HBr (excess), heat; aryl ethers give phenol + alkyl halide
- Epoxide synthesis: RCO3H (mCPBA, peroxyacetic acid) from an alkene; or halohydrin + base (NaOH); Sharpless (Ti(OiPr)4, (+/-)-DET, t-BuOOH), advanced
- Epoxide opening, acidic: H3O+, or HX, or ROH/H+: Nu attacks the more substituted C, anti
- Epoxide opening, basic: RO-, RS-, N3-, CN-, NH3, RMgX, RLi, LiAlH4, R2CuLi: Nu attacks the less hindered C, anti
- Syn-dihydroxylation: OsO4 (catalytic + NMO) or cold dilute KMnO4/OH- gives a cis-diol
- Anti-dihydroxylation: epoxidation (mCPBA) then H3O+ gives a trans-diol
- Oxidative cleavage of diols: HIO4 (periodic acid) or Pb(OAc)4 gives 2 carbonyls
- Pinacol rearrangement: 1,2-diol, H+, heat gives a ketone (1,2-shift)
- Thiol/sulfide (if covered): RX + NaSH gives RSH; RX + RS- gives R2S; oxidation of a sulfide gives a sulfoxide (H2O2 or mCPBA), then a sulfone

Foundations: SN1/SN2/E1/E2 selection; stereochemistry of ring opening (anti; Markovnikov vs anti-Markovnikov regiochemistry); carbon oxidation states; leaving-group generation; protecting-group logic.

## 6. Spectroscopy & Structure Determination

Placement varies widely: front-loaded, distributed, or here. IR / NMR (1H, 13C) / MS; DEPT, COSY, 2D sometimes.

Reactions (reagents / conditions):
- No preparative reactions; an analytical chapter
- IR functional-group diagnostics (C=O about 1700, O-H/N-H broad, C≡N about 2250, sp/sp2/sp3 C-H)
- 1H NMR: chemical shift, integration, multiplicity (n+1), coupling constants J; 13C and DEPT
- Mass spec: M+, isotope patterns (Br, Cl, M+1 for C/N), key fragmentations (α-cleavage, McLafferty), degrees of unsaturation

Foundations: degrees-of-unsaturation calculation; electron shielding/deshielding; functional-group identification; diastereotopic vs equivalent protons; isotope abundances.

## 7. Aldehydes & Ketones: Nucleophilic Addition

Universally covered.

Reactions (reagents / conditions):
- Hydration: H2O, H+ or OH- catalyst gives a geminal diol (favored for formaldehyde, hydrates like chloral, ninhydrin)
- Cyanohydrin: HCN, or NaCN/KCN then H+ (or TMSCN)
- Bisulfite adduct: NaHSO3 gives an α-hydroxysulfonate (purification)
- Acetal / hemiacetal: ROH (1 eq gives a hemiacetal; 2 eq an acetal), dry HCl / TsOH; ethylene glycol gives a cyclic dioxolane (carbonyl protecting group); removed by H3O+
- Thioacetal: RSH or HS-CH2CH2-SH / BF3; then Raney Ni/H2 gives CH2 (desulfurization, another C=O to CH2 route)
- Imine (Schiff base): 1° amine, mild acid pH about 4 to 5, -H2O
- Enamine: 2° amine (pyrrolidine, morpholine), acid catalyst, -H2O (Stork enamine chemistry later)
- Oxime: NH2OH; hydrazone: NH2NH2; 2,4-DNP (Brady's reagent): 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine (orange precipitate test); semicarbazone: H2N-NH-C(=O)-NH2
- Wittig: R3P=CR'2 ylide (from R3P + R'X then base n-BuLi) gives an alkene; stabilized ylide gives E, non-stabilized gives Z; Horner-Wadsworth-Emmons: phosphonate ester/base gives an E-enoate
- Grignard: RMgX then H3O+; organolithium RLi gives an alcohol (1°/2°/3° depending on the carbonyl)
- Reduction to alcohol: NaBH4 (mild, protic OK); LiAlH4 (strong, aprotic, then H3O+); LiAlH(OtBu)3; catalytic H2/Pd or Ni; Luche (NaBH4/CeCl3, 1,2 on enones)
- Reduction to CH2: Clemmensen Zn(Hg)/HCl; Wolff-Kishner N2H4/KOH, heat; thioacetal/Raney Ni
- Meerwein-Ponndorf-Verley (optional): Al(OiPr)3/iPrOH gives an alcohol (reversible with Oppenauer oxidation)
- Baeyer-Villiger: RCO3H (mCPBA, peroxytrifluoroacetic acid) gives an ester/lactone; migratory aptitude 3° > 2° > Ar > 1° > Me
- Oxidation of aldehyde to acid: Tollens Ag(NH3)2+; KMnO4; Jones; Ag2O; H2O2; Benedict's/Fehling's (test)
- Cannizzaro: aldehyde with no α-H + conc. NaOH gives alcohol + carboxylate (disproportionation)

Foundations: carbonyl polarity and sterics; nucleophile strength and hard/soft; reversible-addition equilibria (Le Chatelier); hemiacetal to acetal mechanism; hydride vs carbanion delivery; 1,2 vs 1,4 selectivity on enones.

## 8. Carboxylic Acids & Derivatives: Nucleophilic Acyl Substitution

Universally covered. The reactivity ladder is the organizing principle.

Reactions (reagents / conditions):
- Acid to acyl chloride: SOCl2; (COCl)2 (oxalyl chloride)/DMF; PCl3, PCl5
- Acid to anhydride: heat diacids (cyclic); or acid + acyl chloride; P2O5
- Fischer esterification: RCOOH + R'OH, H2SO4/TsOH (reversible; drive with excess or by removing H2O)
- Acid to ester (other): CH2N2 gives a methyl ester; carboxylate + R-X (SN2); Steglich: DCC/DMAP (or EDC)
- Acyl chloride to ester (R'OH/pyridine), amide (2 R''NH2, or R''NH2 + base), anhydride (carboxylate), acid (H2O)
- Anhydride to ester/amide/acid (analogous, less reactive)
- Ester hydrolysis: acidic (reversible) or saponification NaOH/H2O (irreversible, gives the carboxylate)
- Transesterification: R''OH, acid or base catalyst
- Amide hydrolysis: H3O+, heat or NaOH, heat (forcing)
- Nitrile: from R-X + NaCN (SN2) or dehydration of an amide (SOCl2, P2O5, POCl3); hydrolysis gives COOH; partial hydrolysis gives the amide
- Reductions: LiAlH4: acid/ester/acyl chloride to 1° alcohol; amide to amine; nitrile to 1° amine. DIBAL-H (1 eq, -78 °C): ester to aldehyde, nitrile to aldehyde (imine, then H3O+). NaBH4 reduces acyl chlorides (slow on esters)
- Organometallics on derivatives: 2 RMgX/RLi on an ester or acyl chloride gives a 3° alcohol; Gilman R2CuLi + acyl chloride gives a ketone (stops once); RLi + carboxylate gives a ketone
- Weinreb amide (optional): N,O-dimethylhydroxylamine amide + RMgX/RLi gives a ketone (no over-addition)
- Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky: RCOOH + Br2/PBr3 (cat. P) gives an α-bromo acid
- Decarboxylation: β-keto acids / malonic acids, heat (6-membered TS); Kolbe electrolysis (optional); Hunsdiecker (Ag salt + Br2, optional)

Foundations: reactivity ladder: acyl halide > anhydride > ester ≈ acid > amide > carboxylate; tetrahedral intermediate; leaving-group ability; acid/base effects on nucleophile and substrate; why amides resist hydrolysis and why saponification is irreversible.

## 9. Enols & Enolates: α-Carbon Chemistry

Universally covered; typically the hardest block. Depth of condensation coverage varies.

Reactions (reagents / conditions):
- α-Halogenation (acid): X2 (Br2/Cl2), H+ (mono, via the enol)
- α-Halogenation (base): X2, OH- (poly); haloform: methyl ketone + excess X2/NaOH gives CHX3 + carboxylate (iodoform test)
- HVZ (from Topic 8) for α-halo acids
- Aldol addition: base (dilute NaOH/KOH) or acid gives a β-hydroxy carbonyl; aldol condensation: heat, -H2O gives an α,β-unsaturated carbonyl (enone/enal)
- Crossed/mixed aldol: control via a partner with no α-H, or an LDA-preformed enolate; directed aldol (LDA, -78 °C)
- Intramolecular aldol gives a ring (5- and 6-membered favored)
- Claisen condensation: ester + NaOEt gives a β-ketoester; Dieckmann (intramolecular) gives a cyclic β-ketoester; crossed Claisen (one ester with no α-H)
- Malonic ester synthesis: diethyl malonate + NaOEt + R-X (alkylate; can dialkylate), hydrolyze (H3O+), decarboxylate (heat) gives a substituted acetic acid
- Acetoacetic ester synthesis: ethyl acetoacetate + NaOEt + R-X, then hydrolysis/decarboxylation gives a methyl ketone
- Michael addition (1,4, conjugate): stabilized enolate/amine/thiol/cyanide donor + α,β-unsaturated acceptor (enone, enoate, nitrile, nitro)
- Stork enamine: enamine + R-X or a Michael acceptor, then H3O+ gives an α-alkylated/acylated carbonyl
- Robinson annulation: Michael + intramolecular aldol condensation gives a cyclohexenone
- Enolate alkylation: LDA (kinetic, less-substituted enolate, -78 °C) vs thermodynamic (NaOEt/protic, more-substituted); then R-X
- Mannich: enolizable carbonyl + CH2O + 2° amine (HCl) gives a β-amino carbonyl
- Knoevenagel (optional): active methylene + aldehyde, amine base
- Reformatsky (optional): α-halo ester + Zn + carbonyl gives a β-hydroxy ester
- Conjugate organocuprate: R2CuLi + enone gives 1,4-addition

Foundations: keto-enol tautomerism; α-proton pKa (about 20 ketone, about 11 β-dicarbonyl, about 25 ester); enolate resonance stabilization; kinetic vs thermodynamic enolate; electrophile/nucleophile pairing; retrosynthetic disconnection of C-C bonds; 1,2 vs 1,4 (hard/soft) selectivity.

## 10. Amines

Universally covered.

Reactions (reagents / conditions):
- Reductive amination: aldehyde/ketone + NH3 / 1° / 2° amine + NaBH3CN (or NaBH(OAc)3, or H2/Ni) gives an amine (controls over-alkylation)
- Direct alkylation: NH3 + R-X (over-alkylation problem: mixtures + quaternary salt)
- Gabriel synthesis: potassium phthalimide + 1° R-X (SN2), then hydrazinolysis (N2H4) or hydrolysis gives a pure 1° amine
- Azide route: R-X + NaN3 gives R-N3, then LiAlH4 or H2/Pd or PPh3 (Staudinger) gives a 1° amine
- Nitrile reduction: R-C≡N + LiAlH4 or H2/Ni gives R-CH2-NH2 (adds one C)
- Amide reduction: LiAlH4 gives an amine (keeps the N substitution pattern)
- Nitro reduction: H2/Pd; Fe/HCl; Sn/HCl; SnCl2 gives an amine
- Hofmann rearrangement: 1° amide + Br2/NaOH gives a 1° amine with loss of one C (-CO2)
- Curtius rearrangement (optional): acyl azide, heat gives an isocyanate, then an amine
- Hofmann elimination: exhaustive methylation (excess CH3I), Ag2O/H2O, heat gives the less-substituted alkene (anti-Zaitsev)
- Cope elimination (optional): amine oxide (H2O2), heat gives an alkene (syn)
- Diazonium formation: 1° aryl amine + NaNO2/HCl, 0 to 5 °C gives Ar-N2+
- Diazonium substitutions: H2O/heat gives phenol; CuCl gives ArCl, CuBr gives ArBr (Sandmeyer); CuCN gives ArCN; KI gives ArI; HBF4/heat gives ArF (Balz-Schiemann); H3PO2 gives Ar-H (deamination)
- Azo coupling: Ar-N2+ + activated arene (phenol/aniline) gives an azo dye
- Acylation to protect: amine + Ac2O gives an amide (moderates EAS on aniline)

Foundations: N basicity vs nucleophilicity (aliphatic vs aryl vs amide); the over-alkylation problem; EAS coupling of diazonium (Topic 3 link); elimination regiochemistry (Hofmann vs Zaitsev); 1°/2°/3°/4° classification; rearrangement mechanisms (Hofmann/Curtius, N migration).

## 11. Phenols

Sometimes folded into the alcohols/EAS chapters rather than standalone.

Reactions (reagents / conditions):
- Acidity / salt formation: NaOH gives sodium phenoxide (phenol pKa about 10)
- Williamson: phenoxide + R-X gives an aryl alkyl ether
- Ester formation: phenol + RCOCl/(RCO)2O gives an aryl ester
- EAS on phenol: strongly activated; Br2/H2O gives 2,4,6-tribromophenol; dilute HNO3 gives nitrophenols
- Kolbe-Schmitt: phenoxide + CO2, heat/pressure gives salicylic acid
- Reimer-Tiemann: CHCl3/NaOH gives salicylaldehyde
- Oxidation to a quinone: Na2Cr2O7, Fremy's salt, Ag2O
- Claisen rearrangement (optional): allyl aryl ether, heat gives an o-allyl phenol ([3,3]-sigmatropic)

Foundations: phenol acidity vs alcohols (resonance); strong ring activation toward EAS; phenoxide as a nucleophile; quinone/hydroquinone redox.

## 12. Pericyclic Reactions (Overview)

NOT universally in Orgo II; many programs cover only Diels-Alder (Topic 1) and defer the rest to advanced courses.

Reactions (reagents / conditions):
- Cycloadditions: Diels-Alder [4+2]; [2+2] (photochemical, hν); 1,3-dipolar (azide/nitrile oxide + dipolarophile)
- Electrocyclic: ring opening/closing; thermal vs photochemical gives con/disrotatory (Woodward-Hoffmann)
- Sigmatropic: Cope [3,3]; Claisen [3,3]; [1,5]-H shifts
- A reagent-light chapter, governed by orbital symmetry, not reagents

Foundations: FMO / orbital symmetry; Woodward-Hoffmann rules; thermal vs photochemical selection rules; suprafacial/antarafacial, con/disrotatory.

## 13. Carbohydrates

Frequently deferred to biochemistry; when covered, depth ranges from one lecture to weeks.

Reactions (reagents / conditions):
- Mutarotation: aqueous acid/base; α to β anomer equilibrium via the open chain
- Glycoside formation: ROH, HCl (dry) gives an acetal (glycoside); enzymatic/chemical
- Ether/ester (protecting): (CH3)2SO4/NaOH or CH3I/Ag2O (methylation); Ac2O (peracetylation)
- Oxidation: Tollens/Benedict/Fehling (reducing-sugar test); Br2/H2O gives an aldonic acid (mild, C1 only); dilute HNO3 gives an aldaric acid (C1 and C6); enzymatic gives a uronic acid
- Reduction: NaBH4 or H2/Ni gives an alditol
- Kiliani-Fischer: HCN then hydrolysis/reduction; chain +1 C (epimeric pair)
- Wohl / Ruff degradation: chain -1 C
- Osazone: excess PhNHNH2 (C1 and C2 stereochemistry probe)

Foundations: Fischer projections, D/L; anomers, epimers, mutarotation; hemiacetal/acetal chemistry (link to Topic 7); pyranose/furanose ring forms; reducing vs non-reducing sugars.

## 14. Amino Acids, Peptides & Proteins

Often deferred to biochemistry. Coverage highly variable.

Reactions (reagents / conditions):
- Strecker synthesis: RCHO + NH3 + HCN gives an α-amino nitrile, then hydrolysis gives an α-amino acid
- Gabriel-malonic (Sørensen): diethyl acetamidomalonate route gives an amino acid
- Reductive amination of an α-keto acid gives an amino acid
- Peptide coupling: DCC (or EDC/HOBt); requires N-protection (Boc: (Boc)2O; Cbz; Fmoc) and C-activation
- Deprotection: Boc with TFA; Cbz with H2/Pd; Fmoc with piperidine
- Edman degradation: PhNCS gives the sequence from the N-terminus (one residue at a time)
- Ninhydrin: colorimetric detection (purple; proline yellow)
- Sanger's reagent: 2,4-dinitrofluorobenzene (SNAr) labels the N-terminus

Foundations: zwitterion / isoelectric point (pI) / pKa titration; peptide-bond geometry; protecting-group strategy (orthogonality); SNAr link (Sanger, Topic 4); stereochemistry (L-amino acids).

## 15. Lipids, and Other Optional Capstones

NOT universal; inclusion of lipids, nucleic acids, or a synthesis-design capstone depends on the instructor and time.

Reactions (reagents / conditions):
- Fat/oil hydrolysis: saponification (NaOH) gives soap + glycerol
- Hydrogenation of oils: H2/Pd or Ni gives saturated fats
- Terpene / steroid biosynthesis (conceptual): isoprene units
- Multistep synthesis design: retrosynthesis, protecting-group orchestration (capstone, no new reagents)

Foundations: ester/saponification chemistry (Topic 8); fatty-acid structure and saturation; retrosynthetic analysis; chemoselectivity and protecting-group orthogonality.
