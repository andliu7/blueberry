Q: Why does the thermometer bulb sit level with the side arm?
A: So it reads the temperature of the <strong>vapor</strong> leaving toward the
   condenser rather than the liquid boiling underneath it. Sitting too high reads
   the room, and too low reads the pot.

Q: What is the azeotrope of ethanol and water?
A: $95\%$ ethanol boiling at $78.2^\circ C$. It is the most concentrated ethanol
   simple distillation can reach, because at that composition the vapor has the
   same ratio as the liquid.

Q: Which layer of a fractionating column is richest in the lower boiling component?
- The bottom, nearest the pot
- The middle, where the packing is densest
* The top, just below the side arm
A: The <strong>top</strong>. Each condensation and revaporization up the packing
   is another theoretical plate, so the vapor gets richer in the volatile
   component the further it climbs.

Q: Why does a fractionating column separate better than a simple still head?
A: <strong>Surface area</strong>. The packing gives the vapor somewhere to
   condense and revaporize many times over, and each cycle is effectively another
   simple distillation.

Q: Which of the following will ruin a distillation? Select all that apply.
* Heating so hard the pot boils over into the condenser
- Using a boiling chip
* Distilling to dryness
- Insulating the column
A: A and C. Flooding carries pot liquid straight into the receiver and undoes the
   separation, and distilling to dryness concentrates whatever was left behind
   until it can decompose or worse. Boiling chips and insulation both help.

Q: What does a flat plateau on a distillation curve mean?
A: A <strong>pure fraction</strong> is coming over. The temperature holds while
   one component distills, then climbs again as the pot runs out of it and the
   next component starts to contribute.
